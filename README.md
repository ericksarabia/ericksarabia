<samp>LOG ENTRY ZERO</samp>

I was a kid. Before dawn I woke up, went outside, looked up — and there was a ship. A perfect triangle, flying, trailing light that formed more triangles and faded.

My whole family came out. We stood there watching, and it never left me. This is that ship; the rest is where I have been since.

---

<samp>LOG 01 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; EDUCATION</samp>

### Universidad Politécnica de Pachuca

<samp>Bachelor's Degree in Software Engineering &nbsp;·&nbsp; Sep 2009 — Apr 2013</samp>

I was ten the first time I sat in front of a computer. Then school taught me to program. This was the only thing I ever wanted to study.

I came to learn how software is really made. I left with a job already waiting.

---

<samp>LOG 02 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### Mantiz Game Studios

<samp>Game Developer Intern &nbsp;·&nbsp; Jan 2013 — Jun 2013</samp>

My first job. The chance came and I did not think twice.

For months I learned how software is really built inside a company, which is nothing like a classroom. At the end of it, they asked me to stay.

---

<samp>LOG 03 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### Mantiz Game Studios

<samp>Game Developer &nbsp;·&nbsp; Jul 2013 — Dec 2015</samp>

They kept me, and I spent two and a half years making games. Almost everything I can do today started somewhere in here.

In my last year I built one that ran in a browser. That was the door. I went looking for everything I could find about the web, and I never went back.

---

<samp>LOG 04 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### FINAGAM

<samp>Web Developer &nbsp;·&nbsp; Jan 2016 — Dec 2017</samp>

A finance company with no website at all. Building their first one was the whole job.

Then came the tools around it: calculators where anyone could try out a loan or an investment before talking to us. People used them, and then they called. That is where the web stopped being something I was learning.

---

<samp>LOG 05 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### Televisa

<samp>Frontend Developer &nbsp;·&nbsp; Mar 2018 — Feb 2021</samp>

I knew how to build for the web. Here I found out what that was worth.

Three years on speed, and on the habits that last, next to engineers who had come from Google, on sites that hundreds of thousands of people opened every day. I arrived knowing how to build. I left a frontend developer.

---

<samp>LOG 06 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### Skydropx

<samp>Senior Frontend Engineer &nbsp;·&nbsp; Mar 2021 — Feb 2022</samp>

I went looking for a startup: somewhere smaller and faster, with a lot to learn in very little time.

The work grew past my own screen. That year I built the base the rest of the frontend team would work on top of — the first time what I made was for other engineers instead of for users.

---

<samp>LOG 07 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### Skydropx

<samp>Engineering Manager &nbsp;·&nbsp; Mar 2022 — Feb 2023</samp>

The company was growing fast and they offered me the manager role. I already knew that was where I wanted to go.

Teaching people and leading them are the two parts of this work I love most, and here they were the whole job. I met everyone we hired, and walked each of them through their first days.

---

<samp>LOG 08 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### Danone

<samp>IT Manager &nbsp;·&nbsp; Mar 2023 — Feb 2026</samp>

A big global company, where everything moved slower. That was exactly why I went.

Three years leading projects and people across several countries, at a size where the hardest part is no longer the code. I came for that, and to lead a team in a second language. I left a solid base standing behind me.

---

<samp>LOG 09 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; EDUCATION</samp>

### UTEL Universidad

<samp>Master's Degree in Software Engineering and Management &nbsp;·&nbsp; Jan 2024 — Dec 2025</samp>

A master's degree, earned while I was running a team.

I did it for one reason. Some day I want to teach: to stand in a classroom and hand all of this to someone who is just starting, the way it was once handed to me.

---

<samp>LOG 10 / 10 &nbsp;·&nbsp; WORK</samp>

### GBM

<samp>Engineering Manager &nbsp;·&nbsp; Apr 2026 — Present</samp>

I went looking for the next challenge, and I wanted this one harder than the last. More technical. More complex.

That is what this is: problems I enjoy being stuck on, a new business to learn, and both halves of the job — the code and the people — still to get better at.

<samp>END OF THE LINE</samp>

### The log continues

Still flying. I want to grow here, and build a real community around this work.

The kid sitting down in front of that first computer had no idea any of this was out there. Neither do I, about what comes next. That is the best part.
